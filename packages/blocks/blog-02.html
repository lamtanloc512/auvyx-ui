The quiet return of the hand-drawn interface

After a decade of flat grids, product teams are reaching for texture, imperfection and the marks a person leaves.

MA Maya Alvarez · Nov 8, 2026 · 6 min read

For most of the last decade, the safest thing a product team could ship was a grid of rounded rectangles in a neutral palette. It tested well, it scaled, and nobody was ever fired for it.

The marks a person leaves

What changed was not taste but tools. When every interface can be generated in seconds, the ones that feel made by someone stand out. Texture, a slightly uneven line, a margin note that was never cleaned up: these are signals of attention.

The goal is not to look handmade. It is to look like someone cared which way the line went.
  • Keep one hand-drawn element per screen, not ten.
  • Let it carry meaning: an underline that marks what changed.
  • Draw it at the size it ships; scaled sketches look like clip art.

Where it goes wrong

The failure mode is costume: a sketchy font on a form that is otherwise unchanged. If the structure is still the same grid, the texture reads as a filter.
